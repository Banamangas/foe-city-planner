# Project review — speed, approach, data gaps, city variance (2026-11-11)

Read: all of `tasks/` (lessons, todo, remaining-work, next-things), `foeopt/roads_first.py`,
`router.py`, `validate.py`, `loader.py`, `bounds.py`, `webapp/params.py`. Ran 4 small
experiments (scripts + layouts in `output/objective-probe/`, gitignored).

## TL;DR

1. **The biggest gap: the inner CP-SAT model has no objective.** `probe()` is pure
   feasibility; the road count is whatever `route()` makes of the first placement CP-SAT
   happens to return. The k-walk, seed-polish, the band filter and the "luck of the solution"
   framing are all workarounds for that. Adding the exact in-skeleton objective
   (minimize the skeleton cells actually needed) beat **every stored record I tried, in 120 s each**:

   | skeleton (record file) | record | + objective (120 s, 12 threads, hinted) |
   |---|---|---|
   | darkzig 97 lane k106 | 97 | **90** |
   | darkzig 94 nonuniform k105 | 94 | **91** (93 on a first run) |
   | darkzig 99 lane k105 | 99 | 97 |
   | darkzig 98 lane k105 | 98 | 98 |
   | FR16 76 nonuniform k84 | 76 | **71** |

   All checked: `validate` OK (fillers placed), `exact_route` = route() count and OPTIMAL,
   `is_valid`, 0 rotated, 224/224 and 89/89 buildings placed. **New records, pending
   your review: darkzig 90 (was 94), FR16 71 (was 76).**

2. **Why this was missed:** the 2026-07-21 placement-objective study tested this objective as
   proxy P2 ("subtree") but defined it wrongly. It counts *every* skeleton cell a consumer
   touches, not the *minimum* each consumer needs, so in a dense packing it equals k. I measured
   P2 = 105 = k on the 94-record placement. A constant can't correlate (that's the ρ = −0.05), and
   the study's conclusion ("no proxy works, the ~5-road oracle gap is unreachable") rests on it.
   The real objective takes O(k) booleans plus one implication per anchor option. It is not
   the memory-hungry joint model from next-things #6.

3. **Why good cities vs bad cities** (details below): (a) how much room the input leaves;
   (b) fillers aren't in the CP-SAT model; (c) the skeleton generator ignores building depths;
   (d) symmetry and model size from many identical consumers; (e) presets tuned on 2–3 cities.

4. **Speed:** the time goes to UNKNOWN timeouts (61% of probes in the baseline), not Python.
   Changing the formulation (optimize instead of k-walk, and an encoding without symmetry) is
   worth much more than code-level tuning.

## 1. Was the approach right?

**Decomposition: yes.** Roads-first (fixed connected skeleton, then place) turns the one
global constraint, connectivity to the TH, into something that holds by construction. That
is why it beat every packer, LNS and RL attempt (158 → 94). Seven months of negative results
on the alternatives back this up.

**Inner formulation: no.** Treating each skeleton as a yes/no question and walking k down
costs on three fronts:
- **Wasted budget:** most probes near the frontier are UNKNOWN and burn their full limit.
- **Wasted quality:** for one skeleton, the achieved count varies by up to 10 roads by seed
  (lessons 2026-07-22). Seed-polish samples that spread at random; an objective searches it.
- **Fragile control:** k_start margins, slicing, UNDERSAMPLED and the cliff and monotonicity
  problems exist only because the outer loop must guess the k where feasible meets tight.
  With an objective, pick a *loose* k (feasibility is cheap there: 98% SAT for nonuniform on
  darkzig) and let the solver push the used-road count down. The skeleton becomes a
  superset of roads, not a target.

Note `route()` routes over *all* free cells, so the objective is an upper bound and `route()`
or `exact_route()` can only lower the result.

**Process critique.** The measure-first discipline is excellent and caught many bad builds.
Its failure mode shows up three times in the lessons: **a flawed measurement closes a lever
for good** (P2 here; 30 s probes for the lane family; the k-range error). Before using a null
result to close a direction, check the metric isn't constant on the population, which is the
same "plot the statistic on the candidate pool" rule lessons.md already states.

## 2. Speed

Where time goes, from the logs and code:
- CP-SAT probes: 30–300 s each, mostly UNKNOWN near the frontier. **This is ~all of it.**
- `_anchor_candidates`: ~0.36 s per probe in pure Python, recomputed for every consumer even
  when many share a size (FR24 has 81 3×3s, so one computation would serve all of them).
  `skeleton_score.py` already has a 507× faster bitmask version. Easy, but only ~1%.
- Best-fit filler packing: 0.43 s on darkzig, once per SAT. Negligible.

Levers, by expected value:
1. **Objective instead of k-walk** (above). Fewer probes, each one useful.
2. **Two-phase solve:** the existing fast table-constraint probe finds a feasible placement,
   then the objective model is hinted from it. Measured: the cold one-hot objective model
   found *nothing* in 120 s; hinted, it improves right away. Keep both models.
3. **Symmetry-free encoding for identical sizes.** Instead of one item per building (81
   interchangeable 3×3s give 81! permutations, which kills UNSAT proofs), use one boolean per
   (size class, anchor), `sum = count` per class, and `<= 1` coverage per cell. That is the
   standard position-indexed packing model. It also lets fillers go into the same model cheaply
   (see §3b). The previous symmetry attempt (lex ordering with reified constraints) is a
   different, weaker technique; its negative result does not carry over.
4. **Chain the best placement as a hint** across skeletons and k (cheap, same mechanism).
5. Group `_anchor_candidates` by size and use the bitmask version (minor).

## 3. Why medium, unoptimized cities do well and big, optimized ones don't

| city | consumers | fill | input roads | Σ/2 | input eff. | best | best eff. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| darkzig | 63 | 89.6% | 250 | 114 | 46% | 94 → **90** | 127% |
| FR16 | 56 | 83.3% | 92 | 88 | 96% | 76 → **71** | 124% |
| FR17 | 77 | 87.9% | 127 | 121 | 95% | 115 | 105% |
| FR24 | 146 | 90.2% | 232 | 238 | 103% | none | — |
| user | 82 | 96.6% | 142 | 157 | 111% | never run | — |

**a. Headroom.** darkzig started at 46% efficiency, so any decent method saves 60%. Optimized
cities start at 95–111%. Records run at ~120–127%, so FR17 should land near 97–100 and FR24
near 190. FR17 at 115 is underperforming; FR24 and the user city are untested in practice.

**b. Fillers aren't in the model.** CP-SAT places consumers only; fillers are packed greedily
afterwards. With slack (darkzig 283 spare cells) that works. At 97% fill (user city: 3 spare
cells) a consumer placement that ignores fillers will almost never leave packable leftovers.
SAT_FILLER_FAIL (34% on FR16 before repair) is the visible symptom. Dense cities need fillers
inside the model (position-indexed, §2.3). Also, **production never passes `fillers=` to
`prefilter`** (`roads_first.py:1015`), so the filler-area check remaining-work §2.1 marks
as shipped is dead code on the real path.

**c. The skeleton ignores building depths.** Branch spacing should equal depth A + 1 + depth B.
Modal depths per city: darkzig 4–6 (pitch 9–13), user 4/6 (9), **FR16/FR17/FR24 depth 3
(pitch 7; 62/77 and 108/146 consumers)**. `NONUNIFORM_GAPS = (10, 18)` can never produce 7, so
every strip on a 3×3-heavy city wastes ≥ 3 columns. That's affordable with slack, fatal without.
Fix: sample gaps from sums of real depths in the chosen orientation (no rotation, so
horizontal branches use `length` and vertical ones use `width`).
*Measured: inconclusive.* On FR17 at k=121, both 10–18 and 6–8 went 10/10 UNKNOWN at
30 s × 12 threads. Too short a budget to separate them, which is itself point (d).

**d. Model size and symmetry.** Probe time grows with consumer count (36 s, 95 s, 302 s
logged). Identical consumers are the likely driver: FR17 has 52 3×3s, FR24 81. In this
session FR17 gave **31/31 UNKNOWN** at 30 s across three skeleton sets. The current
item-per-building model can't decide these instances on a user-scale budget. That points
to §2.3, not more time.

**e. Calibration on 2–3 cities.** BEST_PRESET, the k_start margin, the band and road_pressure
were tuned on darkzig + FR16, which happen to agree. FR17 disagreed on three levers. Also,
road_pressure uses Σ/2 as the "need", but good layouts beat Σ/2 by 20%+, so it overstates
pressure on exactly the optimized cities.

## 4. Missing data and rules

1. **Two-lane roads are modelled wrong.** In the game they are 2×2 tiles (darkzig's input has
   4 level-2 cells). The router writes a single 1×1 "level-2" cell, e.g. at (49,21) in the 94
   record, which can't exist. So records with a level-2 consumer undercount by at least 3, and
   later-era cities with many military buildings would be badly wrong. **Needs from you:** the
   exact rule (must the whole path to the TH be two-lane, or just the touching tile?).
2. **Chain/set adjacency is ignored.** darkzig has 7 `guild_battlegrounds_road_to_victory`
   chain buildings. The optimizer separates them freely, which can silently cost in-game
   production. Need: which chains/sets matter and their adjacency rules (`chain_id` and
   `set_id` are loaded but never used).
3. **Road-need is inferred for raw exports.** FR exports have no `needsStreet`, so need is
   guessed from `connected` + current adjacency. A building currently unconnected by mistake
   is treated as not needing a road.
4. **Benchmark coverage:** 5 cities, 2 used for tuning. Need ~10–20 more exports covering
   eras, sizes, 3×3-heavy vs mixed, and dense vs slack, plus ideally a few
   hand-optimized ones with known road counts (like your 142) as targets.
5. **No real lower bound** (adjacency bound gives 21 against a 90 result). An LP relaxation of
   the position-indexed model, or a Σ-frontage bound per depth class, would say when to stop.
6. **User constraints not modelled:** fixed buildings, expansion plans, building priorities.

## 5. What to try, in order

1. **Ship the objective (two-phase) in `probe()`** behind a flag and run the A/B at equal
   wall-clock on darkzig, FR16 and FR17. Expected: records at the user box, plus a much simpler
   controller (loose k, optimize; drop k_start, slicing and polish if the A/B confirms).
   Treat the 90 and 71 as candidates until regenerated from the stored artifacts.
2. **Pass `fillers` to `prefilter`** (one line, sound).
3. **Position-indexed, symmetry-free model** with consumers *and* fillers in one solve: the
   fix for FR17, FR24 and dense cities, and a source of LP lower bounds.
4. **Depth-aware gap sampling** for skeletons; re-test with ≥ 90 s probes or the new model.
5. **Fix two-lane road modelling** once the rule is confirmed; add chain adjacency as a
   constraint or soft objective.
6. **Collect more city exports** before retuning any preset.
7. Minor speed work: anchor candidates grouped by size, using the bitmask path.
